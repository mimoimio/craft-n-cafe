I read through your PDF, including its progression, economy, retention loops, and recommendations.

The document is a breakdown of Quantify, a Roblox game. I wouldn't copy its systems directly into your cafe game. Quantify is built around factory production, rounds, rarity, and collecting power. Your game is about running a cafe and building something that feels like your own.

However, there are some genuinely good design ideas in it.

My recommendation: take its progression structure and player-engagement ideas, not its monetisation or complexity.

# What your game should take from Quantify

## 1. Multiple progression layers

Strong recommendation

Quantify separates its progression into different layers:

- Match cash: temporary spending.
- Qubits: permanent progression.
- Crowns: special earned currency.
- Robux: monetisation.

The important idea is that different currencies have different purposes.

For your cafe game, I'd simplify this considerably:

Cash

Your main currency. Earn by serving customers and operating your cafe.

Existing system

Unlock progression

Spend cash or meet milestones to unlock new furniture, decorations, and cafe equipment.

Existing system

Achievements or titles

Optional recognition for milestones, such as serving 100 customers or earning a large amount of cash.

Future addition

I would not introduce a second or third currency yet. Your game doesn't need that complexity.

## 2. A session loop that gives players a reason to continue

Most important idea

Quantify uses a loop:

Queue → Play → Earn → Upgrade → Play again.

Your cafe game can have a much more natural version:

Serve customers

Run your cafe and earn cash

Collect earnings

Build up your savings

Unlock new content

Buy furniture, equipment and decorations

Improve your cafe

Expand and redesign your space

Return to serving customers with a new goal

This is the most important thing I'd borrow from Quantify's session-retention design. Its progression gives players a reason to repeat the core activity.&#x20;

## 3. Daily quests

Worth adapting

Quantify has three daily quests generated from existing gameplay content rather than requiring entirely new game modes.&#x20;

5_6282869720425178420.pdf



For your game, daily quests could be:

| Quest                              | Example reward |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Serve 15 customers                 | Cash           |
| Earn 500 cash                      | Cash           |
| Have 5 customers served by workers | Cash           |
| Place 10 furniture items           | Cash           |
| Serve 10 customers without leaving | Cash           |

I'd start with just three rotating quests.

One important detail: don't make daily quests so rewarding that players feel they should log in just to claim money instead of actually running their cafe.

Quantify has this exact problem: its daily quest rewards can dwarf ordinary match earnings.&#x20;

## 4. Milestone rewards and titles

Good future addition

Quantify rewards players for reaching milestones and has titles that show off accomplishments.&#x20;

5_6282869720425178420.pdf



You could introduce:

| Achievement            | Reward idea            |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| First customer         | Beginner title         |
| Serve 100 customers    | Cafe regular title     |
| Earn 10,000 cash       | Wealthy title          |
| Hire your first worker | Manager title          |
| Serve 1,000 customers  | Veteran title          |
| Unlock all 30 items    | Master Decorator title |

These are especially suitable because your game already has two different motivations: operating a business and designing a cafe.

You don't need to give every achievement a cash reward. Sometimes recognition is enough.

## 5. Optional long-term goals

Quantify has maps, difficulties, and milestones that encourage players to keep progressing.&#x20;

5_6282869720425178420.pdf



For your game, I'd consider:

- Fully upgrading your cafe.
- Unlocking all 30 building items.
- Hiring all four workers.
- Reaching a lifetime customer milestone.
- Earning a certain amount of lifetime cash.

These are goals built from systems you already have.

I wouldn't add prestige, rebirth, or a second currency just to make the game last longer.

# What I would NOT take from Quantify

This is just as important as what you borrow.

| Quantify feature                | My recommendation |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Multiple currencies             | Don't copy        |
| Gacha and random building boxes | Don't copy        |
| Robux progression shortcuts     | Don't copy        |
| Paid convenience features       | Don't copy        |
| Daily quests                    | Borrow the idea   |
| Milestone achievements          | Borrow the idea   |
| Persistent progression          | Definitely borrow |
| Leaderboards                    | Consider later    |
| Gifting                         | Not necessary     |
| Complex rarity systems          | Not necessary     |

Quantify's own design document identifies problems with its economy, including disproportionately large daily rewards and poorly balanced random boxes. You can learn from those mistakes without inheriting them.&#x20;

# My proposed your game roadmap

This is what I'd actually build, in priority order.

Priority 1 — Before release

Progression balancing

- Review prices of all 30 unlockables.
- Measure average cash earned per minute.
- Adjust cash upgrades so they don't make progression trivial.
- Check whether hiring four workers makes the player earn too much.\

  Priority 2 — Before release

  Testing and moderation
- Test with 3–5 new players.
- Test saving and loading cafe builds.
- Test grid placement and object deletion.
- Check for cash exploits and unauthorized building.
- Ensure players can report abuse.\

  Priority 3 — Optional update

  Retention
- Daily quests.
- Achievement titles.
- Lifetime customer statistics.
- Leaderboards.\

  Priority 4 — Future updates

  More content
- More furniture.
- More cafe themes.
- Additional worker customization.
- Seasonal decorations.

# One thing I particularly want to emphasize

Your game has something Quantify doesn't emphasize in the same way: player creativity through cafe building.

Don't let progression become entirely about earning cash faster.

A player should want to continue because:

- They want a prettier cafe.
- They want to experiment with different layouts.
- They want to build a bigger cafe.
- They enjoy watching customers visit their creation.
- They want to improve their business.

That is a much better foundation for your particular game than endless grinding.

## My final recommendation

I'd keep your first release completely free of monetisation, use cash as your only main progression currency, and focus on balancing the 30 existing unlockables.

Before adding daily quests or achievements, get real players into the game.

I have one question that will significantly change my recommendations:

How does your building system work?

- A. Players have their own plot and can freely build their cafe.
- B. Players purchase a predefined cafe and expand it.
- C. Players have a plot, but must unlock rooms or areas before building.
- D. Something else.

I want to understand this because your building system could be the biggest source of long-term replayability, and I'd rather strengthen that than give you unnecessary simulator features.
