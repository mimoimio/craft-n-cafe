## Your in-run notes (adds.md) vs the data

### 1. Registers feel useless — confirmed, but it is a worker-synergy problem

Register #3 was never bought, even at level 8+ with 3,000 cash available. The player stayed at 2 registers for 107 minutes.

The data also shows the second worker mattered: after Worker #2 was hired at 96:01, customers went from **568 to 671 in 11 minutes** (~9.4/min), versus the 6.27/min run average. Registers cap how many customers can be in the cafe at once; workers exploit that cap. Before the first worker, a second register mostly creates a waiting customer the player cannot serve.

Recommendation: do not slow the spawn loop. Teach the register ↔ worker synergy instead (tutorial or NextGoal line), or move the Register #2 goal until after the first worker. A mechanical fix is possible, but it is a bigger design change.

### 2. Everything non-station unlocks by level 5 — confirmed

The player spent **0** on furniture, decor, walls, seats, or architecture across 107 minutes. The shop's building side stopped offering new goals after level 5, and the NextGoal never pointed at it. Pacing these unlocks and pricing later tiers is the right fix.

### 3. Shop red dots, previous/next, store caching — not covered by the log

These are UI polish tasks. The tab-level red dot needs a per-category aggregate; previous/next in `DetailsOverlay` is straightforward; StorePanel product info should be cached once instead of fetched on every mount.

### 4. Teabags and extra stations are not worth it — strongly confirmed

The player never bought Teabag or Icecream, and only bought 3 stations after the starter set. Stations were **1%** of spend; workers were **56%** and upgrades **31%**.

Run C had 10 stations; Run D had 8. Cash per customer was nearly identical (**49.23 vs 47.77**), so the two extra stations in Run C added almost nothing. Teabag at 2,500 did not compete with ingredient upgrades. This is a real content-value problem, not just player preference.

### 5. Expansion prompt — confirmed

The plot expansion was affordable much earlier but was bought at **59:31**, about 31 minutes after the worker. NextGoal already says "Expand your cafe" after the first worker, but it does not show progress or react to affordability. A one-time prompt when `cash >= 3,000` and the expansion is unlocked would help.

### 6. Worker stat button showing black at $400 — not reproducible from the log

The UI uses `playerCash >= nextEntry.Cost`; the server validates the same `nextEntry.Cost`. The likely causes are a stale cash prop at render time or a double-click race between two rapid upgrade requests. This needs a Studio repro with logging. A safe fix is to read the latest cash from `GlobalState:getState()` at click time and add a pending guard per worker/stat.

### 7. Route insufficient-cash buttons to Shop → Premium; remove standalone StorePanel — agreed

This is a clear UX cleanup. It needs a shared way to request the shop's Premium tab, then remove `StorePanel` from `Main` and change the Get Cash calls in `DetailsOverlay`, `StaffTab`, and `BuildPanel`.

### 8. Upgrades feel okay — confirmed

Ingredient upgrades were 31% of spend and drove steady income growth. No need to change the upgrade feel for soft launch.

### 9. Upgrade images — cosmetic, post-launch

### 10. RecipePanel + discovered drinks slice — currently dead

`RecipePanel` is not mounted and `Sidebar` points at a `"recipe"` panel that never renders. The discovered-drinks slice is not registered. This is a post-launch feature unless you want to finish the index before soft launch.

### 11. Workers stronger than the player — confirmed, no nerf

Two workers handled most of the late run. The player kept upgrading them, which is engaging management gameplay. Leave it for now.

### 12. Upgrading Teabag/Icecream without owning the stations — confirmed bug

At **72:31**, the player bought Icecream 1 and Teabag 2 upgrades for **600** while `station_total_placed = 8` and `ingredient_progress_index = 6`. Those upgrades did nothing because customers cannot order drinks requiring stations the player does not own.

This is the highest-value fix from the run: gate ingredient upgrades on station ownership in both the UI and the server. It removes a cash trap and makes station purchases matter.

## My thoughts

### 1. Free-player progression is healthy

107 minutes, level 13, 32,052 earned, zero purchases. The loop can sustain a long session without monetization. That is a good soft-launch baseline.

### 2. Workers are the real progression system

Workers took 56% of spend and were the main throughput lever. The second worker visibly raised customer rate. That is not necessarily bad — it is the management fantasy — but it means the shop must not undersell stations and building.

### 3. Stations need a value pass, not just a price cut

The current station curve fails the "why buy this?" test. Extra stations add menu options, but ingredient upgrades and workers are more efficient. Options worth considering after soft launch:

- Make higher-tier stations unlock significantly higher-value drink families, or weight order selection toward higher-value drinks.
- Give each station a small global value bonus or unique recipe bonus.
- Reduce late-station prices only after the value problem is fixed; otherwise they remain a trap.

Do not just halve the prices and leave the value gap.

### 4. Fix the upgrade-without-station exploit before soft launch

This is a small, safe change with a clear payoff: players stop wasting cash on upgrades they cannot use, and stations become the gate to their ingredient's upgrade path.

### 5. Building is still underused

Zero spend on furniture/decor is a warning sign for a building-centric game. The immediate fixes are pacing (adds #2) and a decoration goal/tutorial; the bigger fix is a cafe rating/decor bonus post-launch.

### 6. No new tuning run is needed before soft launch

Run D answers the free-player question. The next work should be fixes, not another 107-minute run.
